Run the user's weekly review. The goal is to escape the "everything is 80% done" trap by forcing a decision on every open loop, not by adding new ones.

## Gather

1. Read `CHIEF_OF_STAFF.md`, `MEMORY.md`, and the user's `tasks.md`.
2. Pull every open loop you can find: unfinished tasks, aging follow-ups, drafts that never shipped, commitments made in recent sessions, threads awaiting a reply.
3. For each, verify current state against a real source where you can — a "task" may already be done under a different name.

## Present

List every in-flight item. For each, give your recommended decision and a one-line reason:

- **SHIP** — it's close; here's the last mile. (Offer to drive it closed now.)
- **BLOCK** — it's waiting on someone/something; here's the specific unblock and who owns it.
- **KILL** — it's not going to happen or no longer matters; recommend dropping it and say why.

Lead with your recommendation for each — don't just list status. This is a chief of staff making calls, not a report.

## Close

- Update `tasks.md`: mark what shipped, note what's blocked and on whom, remove what was killed.
- Write a memory for anything durable you learned (a recurring blocker, a changed priority).
- End with the one or two things that, if closed this week, would matter most — and offer to start on the top one right now.

Keep the whole thing tight. A weekly review that takes an hour to read doesn't get read.
